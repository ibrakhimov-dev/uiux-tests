O: A is an immediate response to an action. B is a "you can tap this" sign. D is the ability to go back.

B: Grouping and naming the product's pages
C: Designing the conversation between user and product

C: Visual: "where is the button?"; IxD: "how big is the button?"
D: Visual: "who is the user?"; IxD: "what do they do?"
W: IxD: what happens if I tap the button, how do I know it, what happens if I make a mistake.
O: B is stated backwards. C are wireframe questions. D are persona and scenario questions.

D: Deceptive control

O: B: you touch and hold, and the result is exactly where you touched. C: the system senses or speaks by itself. D isn't a type of interaction but a bad practice.

O: A protects the user from mistakes and doesn't deceive. B is about sameness. D does the opposite — it makes the button visible.
