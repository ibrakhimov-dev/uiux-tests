@1
Q: What is User-Centered Design (UCD)?
A: Design based on the designer's own taste and experience
B: Design done quickly based on the client's requirements
C: Design checked at every step by asking and showing it to the user
D: Design built by copying solutions from popular apps
W: In UCD, every decision comes not from guessing but from observing and asking the user.
O: A is the "it's easy for me, so it's easy for everyone" approach. B: the client doesn't replace the user. D: another app was made for other people.
@2
Q: If a designer draws an app thinking "it's easy for me, so it's easy for everyone", what is the result?
A: A beautiful product that doesn't suit anyone
B: A simple product that is equally easy for everyone
C: A complex product that is easy for experts
D: A plain product that is very quick to build
W: An app drawn for oneself may be beautiful, but it doesn't suit the user.
O: B: one person's taste doesn't suit everyone. C and D: the lesson mentions no such result.
@3
Q: In what order do the 4 stages of UCD go?
A: Specify requirements → Understand context → Design solutions → Evaluate
B: Understand context → Design solutions → Specify requirements → Evaluate
C: Design solutions → Understand context → Specify requirements → Evaluate
D: Understand context → Specify requirements → Design solutions → Evaluate
W: Understand the context → specify requirements → design solutions → evaluate.
O: A: requirements can't be written without understanding the context. B: solutions are designed after requirements. C: the process starts with understanding the user.
@4
Q: What is iteration in UCD?
A: Drawing the design once and never changing it
B: Parts that failed in evaluation become new research questions
C: Handing each stage over to a different designer
D: Drawing several color variants of one screen
W: A wrong decision isn't the end of the work but the start of the next cycle.
O: A is the opposite of iteration. C: it's not about dividing work. D: drawing variants isn't going around the cycle.
@5
Q: A tailor "asks, measures, sews, has you try it on and fixes it". Which UCD stage is "try it on and fix it"?
A: Evaluate
B: Understand context
C: Specify requirements
D: Design solutions
W: Testing the finished thing on the user and finding its flaws is evaluation.
O: B is the asking stage. C is the measuring stage. D is the sewing stage.
@6
Q: What goes in the "We learned" column of the project board?
A: Research questions that are still unanswered
B: Specific design decisions drawn from evidence
C: What we saw or heard, i.e. evidence
D: Personal opinions of team members
W: The board: 1) We need to know — questions, 2) We learned — evidence, 3) Design decision — a decision drawn from evidence.
O: A is the "We need to know" column. B is the "Design decision" column. D: a personal opinion is not evidence.
@7
Q: If what a person says differs from what they do, which is more reliable for a design decision?
A: What the person says — attitude
B: The average of the two
C: The designer's own opinion
D: What the person does — behavior
W: What is said is attitude, what is done is behavior. The second is more reliable.
O: A: people often say things they don't do. B: an average with wrong data is also wrong. C: that is a guess.
@8
Q: During observation, a user couldn't remember something and searched again. Which aspect is this?
A: Perception
B: Cognition
C: Emotion
D: Attitude
W: The lesson's example: what I couldn't remember and searched for again — cognition.
O: A: perception — what I walked past without seeing. C: emotion — where I got annoyed. D: attitude — what the person says.
@9
Q: Which interview question follows the rules?
A: "What's the last thing you started and then abandoned?"
B: "Would you use an app like this in the future?"
C: "Our app is really convenient, isn't it?"
D: "Why do you think people are lazy?"
W: The rule: ask not about the future but about a specific past event.
O: B is a hypothetical question about the future. C puts the answer inside the question. D asks for a general opinion, not a specific event.

Q: Which questions do a persona and a scenario answer?
A: Persona — "what do they do?", scenario — "who?"
B: Persona — "when?", scenario — "where?"
C: Persona — "why?", scenario — "how much?"
D: Persona — "who?", scenario — "what do they do?"
W: A persona is a short portrait of the user; a scenario is a 4–5 sentence story starring the persona.
O: A is stated backwards. B and C: the lesson has no such split.
@11
Q: Which research method answers the question "What do they actually do?"
A: Interview
B: Observation
C: Survey
D: Usability test
W: Observation means silently watching how a person does the task.
O: A answers "Why?". C answers "How many people?". D answers "Can they use it?".

Q: When and why is evaluative research done?
A: After design: to check a finished solution
B: Before design: to find a problem and ideas
C: During design: to choose colors and fonts
D: Instead of design: to give tasks to developers
W: Evaluative research checks a finished solution, for example with a usability test.

@14
Q: What is the template for writing a design decision?
A: Because the designer [thinks], the app [decision]
B: Because the user [evidence], the app [decision]
C: Because the client [demands], the app [decision]
D: Because a competitor has [feature], the app [decision]
W: Every decision rests on evidence that was seen or heard. A decision without evidence is a guess and gets deleted.

Q: In evaluation, the user said the decision "Doesn't fit". What happens next?
A: The decision stays unchanged
B: The decision is slightly adjusted
C: A new research question is written
D: The user is replaced
W: "Doesn't fit" — a new question goes into the "We need to know" column and the cycle starts again.
O: A is for "Fits". B is for "Partly fits". D: you can't replace the user because you didn't like the result.

Q: How is One step different from a simple to-do app?
A: It reminds you of daily chores like "buy bread" every day
B: Every day it gives one small step toward your dream
C: Every day it lets you compete with friends
D: Every day it recommends new apps and games
W: One step means "one step toward your dream every day": it brings you closer to a hobby or dream job.

@17
Q: A team member says: "I think users like dark mode, let's do it." What is the right response?
A: Evidence: the team member is experienced, we can trust them
B: Decision: accepted right away by majority vote
C: A guess: first ask the user and find evidence
D: A guess: but time is short, so let's just do it
W: The rule: we don't guess, we check. "How do you know: did you see it, hear it, or guess it?"
O: A: experience doesn't replace evidence. B: the team's vote isn't the user's voice. D: a decision without evidence gets deleted.

D: "Why did you abandon a similar app?"
W: The answer is put inside the question: the person feels pushed to say "yes".
O: B and D ask about a past event and "why?", so they follow the rule. C is one of the research questions from the lesson.

@20
Q: The teacher (the user) rated a One step decision as "Fits". The team says: "So it fits everyone." According to the lesson's rule, what is right?
A: Right: one rating is enough, the decision is confirmed
B: Wrong: one user doesn't mean everyone
C: Right: the teacher is experienced, their rating counts for all
D: Wrong: the evaluation shouldn't have been done at all
W: Another person would give different answers. That's why the homework is to study a second user.
O: A and C: one person's rating isn't evidence for everyone. D: evaluation is stage 4 of UCD.
